● Send the free5gc logs first, the SMF lines in particular. The RA is created in
  the SMF. gtp5g only sends out an RA that it has been given, so the SMF log
  shows how far the RA got before it stopped.

  In this setup the RA goes through four steps:

  1. The SMF builds the RA in NFs/smf/internal/context/router_advertisement.go.
  2. The SMF sends it over HTTP to the UPF (sendRouterAdvertisementViaHTTPClient
     in NFs/smf/internal/context/upf_ra_client.go).
  3. The UPF passes the RA to gtp5g through a netlink call (netlink is the Linux
     channel between a program and a kernel module). gtp5g handles it in
     gtp5g_genl_inject_ra (gtp5g/src/genl/genl_ra.c:35).
  4. gtp5g wraps the RA in GTP-U and sends it down the tunnel to the gNB and the
     UE.

  The SMF logs every outcome of steps 2 and 3. If gtp5g rejects the RA, the UPF
  sends gtp5g's error text back to the SMF, and the SMF writes that text to its
  own log. So the free5gc log shows where the RA stopped, even when gtp5g is the
  part that failed.
